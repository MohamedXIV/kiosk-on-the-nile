# Gameplay-first overlay budget — approved direction, 2026-11-26

User direction: most management menus may occupy half, three quarters, or more of the viewport. Dialogue and interactive cooking are the frequent, core gameplay interfaces, and must not cover more than one quarter of the screen. Preserve readability and interaction, not blanket scaling.

## Screen classes
- Management: inventory, shops, notebook, settings, recipe discovery/ingredient selection and detailed station care may use larger sheets when explicitly opened. No requirement to force them into a quarter-screen.
- In-world gameplay: dialogue (personal/group/topic/choice/continue/leave and recent-history disclosure) and active cooking controls (real active job/stage, preparation, heat, wait, serving, cancellation) must be small overlays. The player should continue seeing the scene. A large recipe catalog is not the interactive cooking overlay.
- HUD: independent compact persistent information; do not use it to conceal additional dialogue/cooking chrome. If both gameplay overlays appear simultaneously, aim to share the same 25% aggregate budget by routing focus/context rather than stacking two quarter-screen panels. Preserve active jobs and conversations; never cancel them just to make room.

## Hard acceptance
Budget = 0.25 × logical viewport width × logical viewport height. Count the full occupied rectangular footprint of the overlay, including speaker tag, visible actions, disclosure, feedback and any protruding children; for multiple rectangles use their union (do not subtract transparent padding). Full-screen scrims are not allowed for these overlays. Do not count the independent persistent HUD as dialogue/cooking area. No clipping, unreadable font, hidden required commands, fake progress, or forced auto-advance to pass. A tall narrow side card or low wide bottom card may satisfy the area rule; do not confuse area with height. Example envelope (not fixed sizes): 740×300 at 1280×720, 600×140 at 640×540, 600×132 at 844×390. Long authored text can paginate/scroll without changing the underlying beat; all choices must remain reachable, in order, with explicit navigation. Keep body/speech legible, currently target 15–16 logical px with Cairo, and retain 44 logical px interactive targets where feasible; if space is insufficient, restructure into separate reading and choice panes/pages instead of reducing font or hit areas. Physical device touch and safe areas still need testing.

## Dialogue proposal
Display the current speaker and current authored beat, not a permanent transcript. Short choices can share a row; long choices wrap in a bounded list or a separate choice page with explicit more/back navigation. On short landscape, consider text and responses side by side. Preserve existing recent-line access and recorded notebook history; do not assume that the notebook stores every transient line. An exit/leave action remains reachable and localized; no change to Ink, participation, or conversation domain ownership. No new world-space bubble requirement; placement near speaker is an optional later visual refinement.

## Interactive cooking proposal
Separate recipe browsing/configuration from the active workstation overlay. Show the actual device, current recipe/stage, actual timer/progress, and context-relevant next command(s) in a compact tray. Less-frequent controls (ingredient selection, detailed care, variants/additions, cancel) can use bounded secondary pages or deliberate management sheets where appropriate, with clear return and no loss of state. Never duplicate simulation, advance timers from UI, bypass provenance/quality/reservations, or add persistent cookware semantics in this UI issue. All existing heat settings, batch serving/stop, cancellation, and selection paths must remain available. Do not turn a large active-job tab into a purported recipe-management exception.
